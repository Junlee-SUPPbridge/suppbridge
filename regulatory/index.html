Awareness, not filing

Regulatory Awareness Built Into Product Decisions.

Which ingredients are permitted, at what dose, with what claim, on what label — differs by market. We consider that at the formulation and sourcing stage, because retrofitting compliance after development is the most expensive way to find out.

Markets

The five frameworks our work most often sits inside.

Not an exhaustive list of jurisdictions — the markets where most of our clients sell, and the ones where the formulation consequences are most often underestimated.

01

United States — FDA

Dietary supplements under DSHEA.

Ingredients are largely managed through the dietary ingredient notification process, and label claims are governed by structure/function versus disease-claim rules. The practical consequence for a Chinese supply chain is documentation: you need specification and certificate trails that support what the label asserts.

  • Structure / function claims
  • New dietary ingredient
  • cGMP
  • Label compliance
02

European Union — EFSA

The framework that most often changes a formula.

Novel Food classification, the permitted ingredient lists and the health-claims register together determine what can be sold in the EU. This is where a formula that is entirely routine in the US can run into an authorisation pathway in Europe — so it has to be considered before the formula is fixed, not after.

  • Novel Food
  • Permitted ingredients
  • Health claims register
  • Labelling
03

United Kingdom — FSA

Diverged from the EU, still diverging.

The UK maintains its own permitted list and its own approach to claims and novel ingredients since leaving the EU. Products developed for one market do not automatically transfer to the other, and the divergence is still widening — worth planning for a variant rather than assuming a single SKU serves both.

  • UK permitted list
  • Claims
  • Novel food
  • Labelling
04

Australia & New Zealand

Two regulators, two regimes.

Therapeutic goods and listed medicines sit with the TGA in Australia, while food-standard questions are handled through FSANZ. Which regime applies depends on the claims and the dose — and that determination often changes the ingredient specification you need to source.

  • TGA listed medicines
  • Permissible ingredients
  • FSANZ standards
  • Dose limits
05

Canada — Health Canada

Product licensing before sale.

Natural health products require a product licence and a site licence, with permitted ingredient and dose requirements set out in the NHP Ingredients Database. Documentation expectations at the manufacturing end are correspondingly specific, which affects which manufacturers are realistically in scope.

  • NHP licensing
  • Site licence
  • Ingredient database
  • Dose limits
Why It Belongs Up Front

Regulatory questions are formulation questions.

01

Permitted ingredients

An active that is routine in one market may need an authorisation pathway in another. Finding that out after the formula is fixed usually means reformulating — and re-sourcing.

02

Maximum dose

Dose limits differ between markets. A dose that is unremarkable in one place can be the reason a product cannot be sold in another, which changes the whole specification you need to source against.

03

Claims & labelling

What you may say, and how it must appear, varies by market. The label has to be decided alongside the formula, not designed after the packaging is ordered.

04

Manufacturing documentation

Some markets expect a documentation standard at the plant that narrows which manufacturers can realistically serve you. It is better to know that before you shortlist than after.
