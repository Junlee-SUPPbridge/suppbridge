The Real Risk

Finding a Supplier Is Easy. Knowing What You're Buying Is Not.

Finding a supplier is easy.

Knowing who you are actually buying from is much harder.

A polished supplier profile does not tell you everything about the company behind it, the actual manufacturer, the raw material source, or how your production will be managed after the deposit is paid.

That's where SuppBridge comes in.

The "Factory" Isn't Actually the Factory

You are quoted factory pricing, but the company quoted may be an intermediary — or the specific process your product needs may be subcontracted to a plant nobody has shown you.

The Ingredient Isn't What You Expected

Raw material source, extract ratio, standardisation, carrier and grade all change what you actually receive — and the quotation rarely states any of it precisely enough to compare offers.

The COA Doesn't Tell the Whole Story

A certificate is only as good as the sample it describes. Batch correspondence, test method, issuing laboratory and the link between document and delivered goods are where buyers lose control.

The Sample Doesn't Match Production

Samples are frequently made by hand, made in small pilot batches, or made by a different line than the one that will run your order. Approval based on a sample is not approval of your production.

Nobody Is Actually Managing the Project for You

After the deposit, communication slows, questions go unanswered, and there is no independent person in China whose job is to represent your interests rather than the supplier's.

You Can't Check Anything Yourself

Language, distance, time zones and unfamiliar business registries make independent verification impractical from abroad — so decisions get made on trust and hope. That's the gap we close.

Supplier & Supply-Chain Due Diligence

Already Working With a Supplier in China?

If you already have a quotation on the table and want an independent view before committing, we can review it. In practice it is usually the entry point to a wider conversation — formulation, ingredients, manufacturer selection and supply-chain setup.

Anything you already have is enough to start:

  • Supplier link
  • Company name
  • Quotation
  • Product specification
  • COA or certificate
  • Supplier contact
Discuss Your Project

A supplier review can focus on:

  • Company identity — who the contracting entity actually is and how it is registered
  • Manufacturer vs trading company — signals about where production actually happens
  • Product capability — whether your specific dosage form and dose are realistically in scope
  • Certification & document consistency — whether the paperwork holds together and belongs to that entity
  • Specification questions — the gaps where offers quietly differ from each other
  • Obvious red flags — and the questions you should ask before ordering

We do not promise absolute authentication. A review is a structured due-diligence exercise that tells you what could be verified, what could not, and where your remaining exposure sits — so you can decide with better information.
